Homiletical Preaching Frameworks

Compare Expository, Textual, and Topical approaches to preaching

📖

1. Expository Preaching (Verse-by-Verse)

Core Principle: "The biblical passage governs the entire sermon outline." The preacher moves sequentially through a continuous unit of scripture (e.g. Romans 8:1-17), allowing the original author's logic and theological flow to dictate each main point and sub-point.

Structure: Historical/Contextual Introduction → Verse Unit 1 (vv. 1-4) → Verse Unit 2 (vv. 5-8) → Verse Unit 3 (vv. 9-11) → Exegetical Synthesis & Direct Application.

Best for: Preaching through entire books of the Bible, systematic exposition, and deep theological foundations.

📜

2. Textual Preaching (Single Verse / Phrase-by-Phrase)

Core Principle: "A single key text (1 to 2 verses) provides both the theme and the major divisions." Rather than covering a whole chapter, the preacher mines a rich verse (e.g. John 3:16 or Romans 8:1) and derives each sermon point directly from its clauses.

Example (John 3:16): Point 1: The Divine Motive ("God so loved the world") → Point 2: The Sacrificial Gift ("He gave His only Son") → Point 3: The Universal Condition ("Whoever believes") → Point 4: The Everlasting Promise ("Shall have eternal life").

Best for: Evangelistic sermons, communion messages, and memorable anchor texts.

💡

3. Topical Preaching (Thematic Synthesis)

Core Principle: "The topic governs the outline, supported by scriptures across the entire Bible." The preacher addresses a specific question, theme, or doctrine (e.g. "Overcoming Anxiety", "Biblical Stewardship", "God's Grace"), gathering multiple passages from OT and NT.

Structure: Thematic Hook → Biblical Definition (Passage A) → The Spiritual Reality (Passage B) → The Gospel Remedy (Passage C) → Practical Action Steps (Passage D).

Best for: Practical life challenges, doctrinal surveys, and multi-week thematic church campaigns.
